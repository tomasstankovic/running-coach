# Changelog

All notable changes. Versions follow semver; installed copies update when the version in
`.claude-plugin/plugin.json` changes.

## 0.17.0

- **A session can mix kilometres and time.** `tp_structure.py` used to write every step in the
  workout's one unit, so for a km runner a 75 s jog between 1 km reps became "300 m" on the
  watch, and for a time runner the 1 km reps became "4:12". Now each step goes to
  TrainingPeaks in the unit it is written in (`km`/`m` as distance, `seconds`/`minutes` as
  time); the spec's `unit` only sets the workout's primary axis. `workouts.md` says how to pick
  the unit per step: flat reps by distance, hill and trail reps by time, recoveries usually by
  time (or 200 m on a track), warm-up and cool-down in the runner's unit. Strides are now steps
  of the structure (a 15' warm-up is 12' easy + 3 × 20 s / 40 s jog, class `warmUp` so the
  analysis doesn't read them as reps). Suggested from the author's own sessions,
  which had the strides only in the text and the jogs turned into metres.

## 0.16.3

- **The week is planned on the runner's days, not on last week's.** A runner agreed their days
  at the start of the conversation, and the coach planned the week on the days of the previous
  week instead, which had been shaped by a family event. Week planning now starts with
  `week.md` §0: days the runner named in the conversation win, then the profile's weekly
  template; the coach asks every week whether the template holds, and a one-off week doesn't
  change the template. `check_week.py` takes `"days"` and FAILs a run on any other day (Slovak
  day names work too); without `"days"` it warns that the days were never confirmed. New eval
  `week-days-not-copied`: template Tue/Thu/Sat/Sun, last week run on Mon/Wed/Fri/Sat. And
  `week-days-from-conversation`, the reported case in two turns: the runner names their days
  in the first message, the profile has no template, and the plan is asked for in the second
  (2 of 2 runs passed all graders).

## 0.16.2

- **Readiness sees more than last night.** `readiness.py` flags sleep debt (3 of the last 4
  nights under 6 h) even when last night and HRV look fine, and takes `--stress 1-5`, the
  runner's rating of load outside running (travel, work, heat, family; 4–5 is a flag). The
  check-in passes what the runner says about travel or a heavy week into the script. Suggested
  on Reddit: a green HRV after four bad nights and a long flight should not clear a tempo
  session. New eval `en-checkin-travel-sleep-debt` is that case.

## 0.16.1

- **Evals in English too.** English copies of the eight Slovak cases behind the README tables
  (`en-*`, generated by `tests/make_en_evals.py` from the Slovak case, so the runner, data and
  graders stay the same), and language tags on every case: `en`, `sk`, and `readme` for the
  table cases in both languages.
- **Block review runs the block report.** "How did the block go?" now has its own paragraph in
  Review mode (`SKILL.md` "End of a block"): run `block_report.py` first, don't add up the weeks
  by hand, and name the key sessions as counts (on target, missed, under target) with the reason
  for the misses. The evals found the coach counting by hand in 3 of 4 runs and, in English,
  leaving out the counts; after the fix the script ran in 3 of 4 and the counts were in all 4
  (block review 75 → 88 % in English).
- **README: with and without the plugin, in both languages**, run on 5 November 2026 with 2 runs
  per case and arm: plain Claude 67 % (English) and 66 % (Slovak), with the plugin 93 % and 95 %.

## 0.16.0

- **Training on a fixed time budget** (`methodology.md` §11, from a research pass over 58 studies):
  plan from the hours a runner can really give (onboarding asks for them), with an allocation by
  budget (≤ 3, 3–5, 5–7, 7+ h); where an extra hour goes (easy running for a half or marathon,
  about 4 min off a marathon per +10 km a week; quality and strength for 5k/10k); threshold or
  sub-threshold as the default quality and long intervals over sprints; busy weeks keep 2 runs
  with intensity, never easy-only; cheaper volume (commutes, doubles, cross-training) and what is
  not proven for amateurs (Norwegian singles, profile-based emphasis). 11 new sources (S54–S64),
  each DOI checked in Crossref.
- **README: "The research behind it"**: the key studies by topic with their size, and the link to
  all 63 sources.
- Evals: a 3-hour week for a 10 km goal, and a busy month before a marathon (tag `v016`), plus six
  in English (tag `en`): a first chat, a weekly review, a taper in miles, a return from a bone
  stress injury, a trail race from a flat city, and a sub-4 marathon on 4 hours a week.
- Evals for faster runners (tag `performance`): sub-3 and sub-2:45 marathons, sub-1:30 and
  sub-1:20 halves, each checked for an honest prediction, gradual volume, race-specific work and
  the race plan.
- **Trail and mountain races** (`methodology.md` §9): long runs by time toward the race's
  duration, vertical in a flat city (incline treadmill, stairs, car parks, weekend hills),
  power hiking, downhill practice with eccentric strength, and fuel and water for the race's real
  duration, rehearsed in race kit.
- **After an update** (`whats-new.md`): the coach notices a new version at the start of a
  conversation (`Coach version` in the profile), says what is new for that runner, checks their
  plan and forecast against the new rules and proposes changes (made only after a yes), and
  publishes the new page template to the same link (the data stays). A scheduled review does
  the same and asks in its report.
- **Faster runners** (`methodology.md` §5): volume from the runner's best block with deloads said
  in the plan, easy doubles past ~6 runs, two quality sessions (double threshold only as labelled
  practice), the specific phase by distance, the limiter named from two results of different
  length, and smaller gains per block. The goal table now has half sub-1:20 and marathon sub-2:45.
- **The first answer to a time goal** (`onboarding.md` §5): the numbers and a fallback goal, the
  limiter, the block shape with its deload rhythm, the key sessions per phase, a halfway check
  point, a tune-up 2–4 weeks out, the taper, and race pacing and fuelling, then only the questions
  that change the plan.
- **"Today" card at the top of the plan page**: today's session with pace, HR cap and distance,
  this morning's readiness and what it means for that session (the morning brief now writes one
  sentence of `advice` with its readiness row), active pain or illness, and tomorrow.
- **Plan page in the order a runner reads it**: "Where we are" first (the phase, week x of y, what
  the phase is for and what to focus on, its goal from the plan and what comes next; a coach's
  note per phase in `phase_notes`), then readiness next to this week (with next week's key
  sessions), then each race's forecast with its road under it, and only then key sessions, load
  and form, blocks, zones and history. The four number tiles are gone: their content is in the
  phase panel, and the race chips count the weeks.
- **A road to each race.** Every A and B race has its own ~5 milestones (4-6) from the 12 weeks
  before it, tagged with the race: for a marathon the marathon-pace long runs, the longest run and
  one with race fuelling; for a half the threshold check and race-pace sessions. Races are the end
  of a road, no longer milestones. `season_plan.py roads` sets them on an existing plan, keeping
  every result; `check-roadmap` warns when a road has fewer than 4 or more than 6.
- **Key sessions on the plan page**: "Next" is only this week and the next, with the next
  milestone after them; "Done" the sessions run, newest first; "Whole plan" everything. Below,
  each race's road on a time line (✓ / ◐ / ✕ / –, the next one marked, the race and its forecast
  at the end). The prediction card shows one line ("milestones 0 of 5 · next …") instead of a list.
- **Projection "if the plan happens" no longer undersells the plan.** Two fixes, found on a real
  season where the projection came out slower than the runner's own best at the same load:
  - Without `weekly_tss` the plan's TSS was rescaled by CTL x 7, which lags behind the load in a
    build and made the plan ~10 % lighter (race-day CTL 42 instead of 47). The plan's own
    estimate is now used, and the skill always passes `weekly_tss`.
  - The time-vs-CTL slope is fitted twice, with today's level and from the earlier races alone. A
    runner who is already faster at a lower CTL than last season made the single fit flat (0.09 %
    per CTL point), as if more training bought little; the middle now uses the mean of the two.
  - The marathon card's "today" mark shows today's form, not the forecast at the plan's volume.
- **A faded marathon and race checks.** `analyze_session.py` prints a marathon's pace to km 30 and
  after it. In the forecast history that marathon gets `steady_pace`: the projection's slow end
  assumes the fade comes back, the fast end that it doesn't, and the middle starts at half of it
  (it used to assume the full fade, which at ~60 km a week is typical, but trainable). The race's
  milestones, read from the plan with the review's `actual.check`, move the middle: every one held
  toward the fast end, every one faded back.
- **Plan page predictions read at a glance**: one headline time with its label above it ("on race
  day, if the plan happens") and one range; today's form and the goal are marks on a time line
  instead of a second range, and the goal's distance from the best case is in seconds or minutes
  ("25 s faster than the best case: a stretch"), not a percentage against "the projection".
- Race plan: weather typed in by the runner (instead of fetched) is shown as "given" with the
  heat line; it used to read "no weather data" although the heat was counted.
- **README**: a picture of three real answers at the top (morning brief, race plan, session
  analysis; fictional runner), a one-minute install, and a table of what the plugin adds over
  plain Claude, from the evals.

## 0.14.0

- **The runner's own zones are the default**, whatever the method (TrainingPeaks and the watch:
  80/20, Friel, 5-zone, ...). `zones.py show` reads any TP method and gives each band its use
  from where it sits relative to the threshold; a heart-rate set with another method than the
  pace set gets its own table. The profile records it (`Zones: own set (TP = Garmin) ...`).
- **Other methods to compare** (`--method`): 80/20 from thresholds, % max HR, heart-rate reserve
  (Karvonen), Daniels VDOT paces (E/M/T/I/R from a race, Daniels-Gilbert equations). Friel is read
  from TP or the watch rather than computed (his published percentages differ between versions).
- **Personal aerobic threshold** (`zones.py lt1`): flat steady runs of 45+ min under 5 % decoupling
  were at or below it; the highest proven HR becomes the easy cap instead of the population 83 % of
  LTHR (with a drift test to confirm big changes).
- **The watch**: its own threshold detection counts as evidence in `zones.py suggest`; after a
  change the runner is told which values to set on the watch (this skill can't write to Garmin).
- `methodology.md` "Zone methods"; sources S46–S50 (Daniels, Karvonen, 80/20, Friel, drift test).
- Fix: the 7-zone X/Y model is 80/20 Endurance (Fitzgerald), not Friel's.

## 0.13.0

- **Training zones** ("Tréningové zóny"): `zones.py show` puts pace, % of threshold, heart rate,
  power and what each zone is for in one table, from TrainingPeaks' own bands (the run set when
  there is one) or from thresholds. The plan page has a training zones panel with the current
  table, power, the easy-run HR cap and how the threshold moved over the season (`zones`
  collection).
- **Keeping them current**: checked in the first review of every month and after new evidence (a
  flat race, a 30-min test, threshold sessions clearly faster or slower than the zones, a new max
  HR, resting HR, power efforts). `zones.py suggest` proposes old -> new with what it means for
  the paces; it never averages sources more than 3 % apart, ignores changes under 1.5 % and hilly
  or hot races, and asks for a retest after 12 weeks without evidence.
- **Writing to TrainingPeaks** after the runner's yes: threshold pace, LTHR / max / resting HR,
  rFTP (`tp_update_speed_zones`, `tp_update_hr_zones`, `tp_update_ftp`), re-read to confirm, then
  the key sessions re-target and the page updates. Creating a missing run power set is offered,
  never done unasked. The scheduled weekly review includes the monthly check.
- Eval `zones-after-race`.

## 0.12.0

- **The coach designs the key sessions; the script computes and checks.** Tested blind on three
  fictional runners who needed adapting (Achilles history before a marathon, no flat ground for a

  and never the week after a race, milestone count.

- Reviews: after a session under target or missed, `actuals` only proposes holding back

- Draft fixes: no quality 2–3 days after races and tests, big marathon-pace long runs never in
  consecutive weeks or before a B race, half-marathon pace never drops from build to specific

## 0.11.0

- **Key-session roadmap** (`season_plan.py roadmap`, `key_sessions.py`): every week of the season
  gets its key sessions from progression ladders by phase and goal distance (more reps, then
  longer reps, then faster), placed on the runner's quality and long-run days, with targets as
  % of threshold speed and paces from `paces.py`. Race-pace targets use the goal pace, at most
  3 % faster than the threshold supports. 6–10 milestones: threshold check, longest run, B races,
  marathon-pace long runs and a continuous 18–23 km at marathon pace 2–4 weeks before a
  marathon, a dress rehearsal for shorter goals, the A race.
- **Results against the map**: `actuals --sessions` matches `analyze_session.py --json` rows to the
  key sessions and judges them (on target, better, under, missed). After under or missed, the
  rest of that ladder moves back a step, logged. `retarget` recomputes future paces after a new
  threshold or goal and logs what moved. `replan` keeps the roadmap.
- **Plan page**: a key sessions section (next + milestones, milestones only, all), by phase, with
  target pace, HR cap, result and verdict, and an "adjusted" mark that leads to the change log.
- `workouts.md` "Progressions", `season.md` §2b, Week mode starts from the week's key sessions.
  Proposed in a coaching session on real data.

## 0.8.1

- **Fix: session analysis on real watch files.** Checked against nine real TrainingPeaks sessions:
  `analyze_session.py` counted every lap as a rep. A 2 × 10 min tempo came out as 6 reps (the 1-km
  auto-lap split each rep), 6 × 2 km as 12, a 10-min Z2 build-up counted as a rep, and mixed sessions
  (2 km + 1 km + 2 × 400 m) got a "pushed the end" verdict across different kinds of rep. Now laps
  inside one workout step are joined, easy Active steps are listed as easy parts, reps are judged per
  kind, strides get one line, short reps skip HR checks, and short-rep pace comes from the lap's
  average pace instead of the rounded distance. Seven new tests cover these cases.
